A repair means someone else handles your phone, and sometimes asks for your passcode. A few minutes of preparation protects both your data and your privacy.

## 1. Make a backup

Repairs sometimes end with a replacement device or an erased phone. Back up your iPhone to iCloud or a computer before you go. Apple's guide to [preparing for a service appointment](https://support.apple.com/109519) lists the current steps.

## 2. Export your vault separately

Hedgehog Bits is deliberately **not** included in iCloud or computer backups, so a normal backup won't save it.

1. Open the vault and choose **Settings → Export Backup…**
2. Save the `.hhbits` file to a Mac, a USB drive or another place off the phone.

If the phone comes back erased or replaced, install Hedgehog Bits and choose **Restore from Backup…**

## 3. Decide whether the vault should be on the phone at all

If you would rather the vault wasn't on the device while it is out of your hands:

1. Export a backup and confirm the file is saved.
2. Use **Settings → Erase Vault** in the app.
3. Restore from the backup when the phone is back.

This is the most reliable option. Nothing is there to be found.

## 4. Think before sharing your passcode

Technicians sometimes ask for your device passcode to test the phone. Your passcode opens your Photos library, messages, email and most apps.

It does **not** open Hedgehog Bits. The vault uses its own password and its own encryption, so the device passcode is not enough to read it. That said, the safest approach is the one in step 3.

## 5. Follow Apple's Find My guidance

Apple may need Find My turned off before a repair, or may offer a repair mode that keeps it on. The steps depend on your iOS version, so check [Apple's article](https://support.apple.com/109519) at the time. Turn Find My back on when you get the phone back.

## 6. When it comes back

- Check that your apps and data are as you left them.
- Restore your vault if you erased it.
- Change your device passcode if you shared it.
