Most of us carry copies of important documents on our phones: a passport scan for travel, a prescription, test results, a tax return, a lease. These files are useful to have with you and valuable to anyone who wants to impersonate you.

## Where these files usually end up

- **The photo library**, if you took a picture of the document. From there it may sync to iCloud, appear in Memories or show up on other devices.
- **The Files app or a cloud drive**, if you scanned or downloaded it.
- **Email and messaging apps**, if someone sent it to you.

None of these are bad places in themselves, but they are designed for convenience and sharing, not for keeping a document private.

## A simple approach

1. **Capture straight into the vault.** Hedgehog Bits has its own camera, so a photo of your passport goes directly into the vault without being saved to your photo library.
2. **Import existing files.** Bring PDFs and other documents in from the Files app.
3. **Delete the originals.** Remove them from Photos or Files, then empty *Recently Deleted* in both apps.
4. **Organise with folders.** For example: *ID*, *Medical*, *Tax*, *Home*.
5. **Export a backup.** These documents are hard to replace. Keep an encrypted backup somewhere safe off the phone.

## When you need to share a document

Sometimes you have to send a scan to a landlord, an employer or a doctor. From the vault, you can share a file to another app. To do that, Hedgehog Bits creates a temporary decrypted copy and deletes it when you close the preview and whenever the vault locks.

The receiving app, and the person you send it to, will then have their own copy. Share only what's needed, and prefer secure channels where you can.

## Notes are documents too

Account numbers, insurance policy details, recovery codes for important accounts: many people keep these in a notes app. Hedgehog Bits includes encrypted notes, saved inside the vault, so they don't sync to iCloud with your other notes.

## What a vault doesn't replace

- **Official originals.** Keep physical documents and official copies where they belong.
- **A password manager** for website logins. It is designed for filling passwords in apps and browsers.
- **Your provider's records.** Medical and tax portals keep their own copies, and those are often the authoritative version.
